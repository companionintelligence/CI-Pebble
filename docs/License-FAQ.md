# License FAQ

### Can you summarize what Companion Pebble's license allows?

The license grants free rights to use, modify, and redistribute Companion Pebble source code for personal or nonprofit purposes. The intent is to stay permissive for those uses while limiting commercial abuse of Companion Intelligence products.

Companion Pebble turns a Pebble smartwatch into a wearable interface for Companion Memory (CI-Server) and a local Ollama LLM: a small watch-side C app talks over Bluetooth AppMessage to a PebbleKit JS phone companion, which relays requests to Companion Memory's REST API and Ollama's chat endpoint.

Companion Pebble uses the [PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0](https://polyformproject.org/licenses/noncommercial/1.0.0). The full text is in [`LICENSE.md`](../LICENSE.md).

### What freedoms do you have as a Companion Pebble user?

If you use Companion Pebble for personal or nonprofit purposes, you can view, modify, and redistribute the source under the same license terms, subject to the PolyForm Noncommercial restrictions.

### Can I fork Companion Pebble?

Yes. For noncommercial use, you can fork, modify, and redistribute Companion Pebble under the same license.

### Can I contribute to Companion Pebble?

Yes. Open a pull request on this repository.

### Can I sell Companion Pebble?

No. Selling Companion Pebble, including cloud-hosted instances, appliances that ship it, or paid support for those products, is not permitted. For commercial partnership questions, email partner@companionintelligence.com.

### Does this license apply to the rest of Companion Intelligence?

Companion Portal stays a separate commercial product — this license does not apply to it.

Companion Hub, Companion Memory, Companion Marketplace, Companion OS, Companion Capture, Companion Browser Extension, Companion OMI, Companion Mentra, Companion Even Realities, and Companion Home Assistant Voice PE use the same PolyForm Noncommercial terms in their own repositories.

Companion Pipe and Companion MaixCAM are still under legal review for third-party licensing terms in code they bundle — don't assume they're covered by this FAQ until that review lands.
